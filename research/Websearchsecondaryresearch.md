# Secondary research: discovery-question answers

Research date: 15 August 2026. Each answer is a secondary-evidence synthesis, not a validated insight about our eventual users. “Primary-research gap” means the evidence cannot answer the question for a specific target segment. Full source register: [WebsearchsecondaryresearchSOURCES.md](WebsearchsecondaryresearchSOURCES.md).

## 1. Ecosystem and boundaries

1. **Question:** Who are the relevant participants?  
   **Answer:** Learners, employers, managers, L&D/HR, IT/security/legal, learning providers, creators, mentors/peers, tool vendors, and hiring/credential actors all affect access, learning, application, or proof. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
2. **Question:** Who experiences, influences, or pays for the problem?  
   **Answer:** Learners experience the capability gap; managers and employers influence time, tools and permission; employers/L&D may pay; tool and course vendors benefit from activation and retention. Exact payer requires primary research. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
3. **Question:** What does “non-technical” mean?  
   **Answer:** It should not be treated as a single skill level: OECD distinguishes advanced AI skills from the much broader need for digital, data-use and interpretation skills. Segment by task and baseline capability, not title alone. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
4. **Question:** Which outcomes matter to each participant?  
   **Answer:** Learners seek career mobility and useful performance; employers seek transformation, productivity and reduced skills gaps; providers seek engagement and completion; governance teams seek safe use. [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)
5. **Question:** Where does the learning journey begin and end today?  
   **Answer:** Evidence suggests it commonly begins with a workplace/career trigger and ends only when learning transfers into work; transfer depends on learner, design and workplace conditions. Map the actual journey in interviews. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
6. **Question:** Which parts occur alone, at work, in communities, or education?  
   **Answer:** Formal content can be self-directed or employer-assigned, while application and manager support occur in the work environment; no single source determines the mix for this target. [Blume meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880)
7. **Question:** Which external forces change the need?  
   **Answer:** Technological change and AI adoption are major forces: WEF reports employers expect substantial skill change by 2030 and ranks AI/big data and technological literacy among rising skills. [WEF PDF](https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf)
8. **Question:** What must be true for “confidently understand, use and build”?  
   **Answer:** A defensible operational definition needs observable tasks: explain a concept, safely apply a tool to a relevant task, and independently assemble a simple workflow/product. Existing pathways similarly separate understanding, applying and building. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
9. **Question:** Are understand, use and build separate or sequential?  
   **Answer:** They are best treated as progressive but not universally required stages: LinkedIn’s framework explicitly sequences understanding, applying, building, then specialist levels. Validate whether each target role needs all stages. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
10. **Question:** Which outcomes are valuable but unnecessary for everyone?  
    **Answer:** Advanced model training and specialization are unnecessary for most workers; OECD says fewer than 1% need advanced AI skills, while broad digital/data/interpretation capability matters more widely. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)

## 2. Learner segmentation

11. **Question:** Which segments have strongest need?  
    **Answer:** Secondary evidence supports prioritising knowledge-work roles with immediate AI exposure, but cannot rank the case-study roles by pain. Microsoft reports strong non-technical AI-learning demand; primary research must identify frequency and severity. [Microsoft/LinkedIn](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part/)
12. **Question:** How do needs differ by role?  
    **Answer:** Needs should be task- and risk-specific: marketing/content, HR/privacy, finance/accuracy, operations/workflows, and product/technical trade-offs differ. Existing market offerings already segment pathways by role. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
13. **Question:** Which segment has frequent enough pain for a habit?  
    **Answer:** Likely segments have recurring, AI-addressable work tasks, but frequency is not available in global reports; test task frequency and repeat intent by role. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
14. **Question:** Who has urgency, time willingness, and willingness to pay?  
    **Answer:** Career pressure and employer adoption suggest urgency, but willingness to pay is not established by this evidence; interview both self-funded learners and budget holders. [Microsoft/LinkedIn](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part/)
15. **Question:** Who has lowest confidence, and why?  
    **Answer:** No reliable source identifies this exact subgroup. Measure confidence alongside prior tool use, jargon comprehension, access, and past technical-learning experiences; do not infer it from occupation. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/bridging-the-ai-skills-gap_66d0702e-en.html)
16. **Question:** Career mobility versus immediate help?  
    **Answer:** Both exist: Microsoft reports workers view AI skills as important for competitiveness and job opportunities, while employer reports frame skills as adoption constraints. Segment by the triggering job-to-be-done. [Microsoft/LinkedIn](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part/)
17. **Question:** Who uses AI casually but not reliably at work?  
    **Answer:** This is a high-value segment to identify; workplace AI use is widespread, but the data do not establish reliable task performance. Test with a recent-task walkthrough, not self-report alone. [Microsoft/LinkedIn](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part/)
18. **Question:** Who understands concepts but struggles to execute?  
    **Answer:** This is plausible wherever learning transfer is weak; systematic reviews identify training design, learner characteristics and the work environment as distinct transfer factors. Identify it with task-based assessment. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
19. **Question:** Who completes tutorials but cannot solve a real task?  
    **Answer:** Completion is not evidence of transfer; transfer research recommends observing use in the target context. This is a primary-research segmentation question. [Ford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/piq.20108)
20. **Question:** How do work environments differ?  
    **Answer:** Tool access, governance, manager support and task context are likely to differ across firms and freelancers; OECD specifically notes skills barriers are acute for many SMEs. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
21. **Question:** What causes “not for people like me”?  
    **Answer:** This needs qualitative inquiry; digital literacy, attitudes and learner characteristics are recognised transfer factors, but no source here establishes individual identity narratives. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
22. **Question:** What constraints exist?  
    **Answer:** Access and organizational support are material constraints; WEF notes not all workers have access to training, while workplace support predicts transfer. Measure time, language, device and policy constraints directly. [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-whats-shaping-the-future-of-the-global-workforce/)
23. **Question:** Who abandons early, and why?  
    **Answer:** Early behavioural traces—unique actions, time, forum participation and action plans—predicted dropout in one small professional-learning pilot; validate on this audience. [Frontline Learning Research](https://doi.org/10.14786/flr.v13i4.1351)
24. **Question:** Who creates workarounds?  
    **Answer:** Identify users who combine courses, AI assistants, colleagues and templates; secondary evidence cannot quantify this workaround behavior for the target. [Microsoft/LinkedIn](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part/)

## 3. Learner problem discovery

25. **Question:** What triggers learning?  
    **Answer:** Likely triggers are career competitiveness, a workplace task, or employer adoption; Microsoft reports strong perceived career relevance. Confirm the exact triggering event in recent-behaviour interviews. [Microsoft/LinkedIn](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part/)
26. **Question:** What job/task/risk is being addressed?  
    **Answer:** Evidence supports skills gaps as a transformation barrier, but the actual task is segment-specific. Ask for the last task, its stakes, current workflow, and desired outcome. [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)
27. **Question:** What do learners think they need first?  
    **Answer:** Do not assume their perceived need equals the needed capability. Existing platforms offer role pathways because selecting content is itself a problem. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a1429531)
28. **Question:** What do they actually need?  
    **Answer:** OECD indicates most workers need general digital/data/interpretation capability rather than advanced AI specialization; exact task skill must be assessed in context. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
29. **Question:** Where do learners get stuck?  
    **Answer:** Secondary evidence says transfer is shaped by digital literacy, learner characteristics, training design and environment; it cannot rank individual failure points. Instrument the journey and observe task attempts. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
30. **Question:** What makes AI intimidating?  
    **Answer:** No single universal cause is established. Test jargon, perceived risk, past failure, low self-efficacy and lack of safe practice separately; do not collapse them into “intimidation.” [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research?hub=83250)
31. **Question:** Which concepts/interfaces cause friction?  
    **Answer:** This must be observed in usability tests. OECD’s distinction between general and advanced AI skills suggests that foundational digital/data concepts are a sensible first test. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
32. **Question:** Which misconceptions block progress?  
    **Answer:** Likely misconceptions include “I must code” or “AI output is reliable,” but they are hypotheses, not findings. Test beliefs before and after a real task. [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research?hub=83250)
33. **Question:** Why does abundant content not create capability?  
    **Answer:** Training transfer depends on more than content: design, learner factors and organizational environment all matter. Content abundance alone therefore cannot guarantee application. [Blume meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880)
34. **Question:** What happens after a course/tutorial?  
    **Answer:** Do not assume application follows completion. Measure the next action, attempted work task, help-seeking and return; completion is a weak proxy for transfer. [Ford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/piq.20108)
35. **Question:** What prevents work application?  
    **Answer:** Supportive work environments and supervisor support are established correlates of transfer; lack of approved tools, relevance or safe practice may also block it. [Ghosh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1108/JWL-08-2016-0079)
36. **Question:** How do learners know what is next?  
    **Answer:** Existing providers use learning paths and role guides, showing the market already addresses sequencing; interview users to find whether such guidance is trusted and sufficient. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/learning)
37. **Question:** What happens after a failure?  
    **Answer:** The evidence supports feedback and self-regulation as useful learning mechanisms, but recovery behavior must be observed. Ask users to replay a recent failed attempt. [EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/evidence-reviews/metacognition-and-self-regulation)
38. **Question:** What support is sought, from whom, and how fast?  
    **Answer:** This is a communication-flow research gap. Map help-seeking across AI assistants, peers, managers, mentors and tool support, including response time and outcome. [Microsoft](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/final/en-us/microsoft-product-and-services/ai/pdf/executive-summary-work-trend-index-annual-report.pdf)
39. **Question:** What is a successful session?  
    **Answer:** Define success as a demonstrated, relevant action plus understanding of why it worked—not merely watching or clicking through content. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
40. **Question:** What creates real capability?  
    **Answer:** Practice, retrieval, feedback and application are stronger evidence-backed candidates than passive rereading; test independent task completion as the outcome. [ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1290236.pdf)
41. **Question:** What causes return?  
    **Answer:** Timely feedback can improve completion, and relevance/application may reinforce motivation, but the actual return trigger needs product testing. [ACM Learning@Scale](https://doi.org/10.1145/3430895.3460991)
42. **Question:** What causes churn?  
    **Answer:** In a small professional-learning study, low early activity and lack of action planning predicted dropout; treat this as a signal to test, not a universal explanation. [Frontline Learning Research](https://doi.org/10.14786/flr.v13i4.1351)
43. **Question:** What AI risks do learners perceive?  
    **Answer:** Privacy, IP, security, bias, reliability and accountability are genuine GenAI risk categories, so perceived risk is credible but must be measured by user and role. [NIST GenAI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
44. **Question:** What creates trust in AI-led learning?  
    **Answer:** Trustworthy design requires valid/reliable, safe, secure, transparent, privacy-enhanced and fair systems; learners should also be able to challenge output and obtain human help. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/ai-risk-management-framework-faqs)

## 4. Employers and managers

45. **Question:** Which employers need AI capability?  
    **Answer:** Need is broad: WEF reports skills gaps are a major business-transformation barrier and OECD reports skills constrain AI adoption, particularly for many SMEs. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
46. **Question:** What business problem do employers solve?  
    **Answer:** Reported motivations include workforce transformation, capacity, productivity and innovation, alongside risk management; determine the dominant buying job in target interviews. [Microsoft](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/final/en-us/microsoft-product-and-services/ai/pdf/executive-summary-work-trend-index-annual-report.pdf)
47. **Question:** Which teams must use AI versus experiment?  
    **Answer:** There is no universal answer; adoption and governance vary. Use role/task mapping and approved-tool lists per employer. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
48. **Question:** How do managers assess gaps?  
    **Answer:** A manager should assess observed task performance, judgment and safe use rather than course completion. Existing providers offer role/skill frameworks, but local performance standards are needed. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a1429531)
49. **Question:** What counts as proof of capability?  
    **Answer:** Best candidate evidence is an independently completed role-relevant task, with quality/risk review; credentials may signal learning but are not sufficient proof. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
50. **Question:** What concerns do managers have?  
    **Answer:** Quality, data privacy, security, IP, bias, accountability and reliability are recognized GenAI governance concerns. [NIST GenAI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
51. **Question:** What incentivises manager support?  
    **Answer:** Managers benefit when transfer improves team performance, but time pressure can conflict. Supervisor support is empirically associated with stronger training transfer. [Ghosh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1108/JWL-08-2016-0079)
52. **Question:** How do policy, access and budget affect learning?  
    **Answer:** Governance requirements can restrict data and tools; NIST recommends connecting GenAI processes to existing IT, legal, compliance and risk management. [NIST GenAI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
53. **Question:** Can learners apply skills immediately?  
    **Answer:** This is employer-specific. It should be screened before recruitment, because workplace opportunity and support influence transfer. [Blume meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880)
54. **Question:** What are safe workplace practice tasks?  
    **Answer:** Start with bounded, reversible tasks using non-sensitive or synthetic data and a human review step; exact task depends on policy and role. [NIST GenAI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
55. **Question:** How do managers communicate expectations and feedback?  
    **Answer:** Organizations can assign/recommend learning through admins and track completion, but completion tracking is not task feedback. Map manager channels and feedback artifacts. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a700799)
56. **Question:** What follows an AI mistake?  
    **Answer:** Responsible practice requires documented monitoring, response processes and human oversight; incident reality varies by employer. [NIST GenAI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
57. **Question:** Who owns capability development?  
    **Answer:** Ownership is cross-functional: OECD calls for shared responsibility across employers, workers and governments, while governance links IT/legal/risk. Determine the actual decision-maker per company. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
58. **Question:** Would employers pay, and for what?  
    **Answer:** The evidence supports a skills-gap business case but not specific willingness to pay. Test purchase against measurable application, adoption, quality or risk outcomes. [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)

## 5. Providers and creators

59. **Question:** Who teaches this audience?  
    **Answer:** The market includes course platforms, universities, bootcamps, creators, employers and tool vendors; LinkedIn is one documented provider combining content, paths, practice and credentials. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/learning)
60. **Question:** What incentives shape provider content?  
    **Answer:** Product pages show providers compete on course breadth, role guidance, practice and credentials; commercial incentives may favor scalable content. Validate provider economics directly. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/learning)
61. **Question:** How do providers choose topic/format/difficulty?  
    **Answer:** Leading providers describe using role, industry, level, skill and tool needs to configure journeys. Investigate whether this matches actual learner needs. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/learning/a-practical-guide-to-upskilling-your-organization-on-ai/build-customized-role-based-ai-learning-journeys)
62. **Question:** What outcomes do providers measure?  
    **Answer:** Available product evidence shows progress/completion and credentials; this research found no general evidence that providers consistently measure workplace transfer. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a700799)
63. **Question:** What blocks applied, role-specific learning?  
    **Answer:** Application requires contextual tasks, feedback and access to a work environment—factors outside scalable content production and known transfer determinants. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
64. **Question:** How do creators keep content current?  
    **Answer:** Providers continuously add/update pathways and courses, but rapid tool change creates maintenance pressure. Document update cadence and obsolete content in competitor research. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/customer-success-center/product-updates/all-updates/q1-26)
65. **Question:** What is needed for interactive practice?  
    **Answer:** Practice needs a task environment, feedback, assessment, contextual data and safeguards; AI role play/coaching is one current implementation. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/customer-success-center/product-updates/all-updates/q1-26)
66. **Question:** Where do providers lose learners?  
    **Answer:** Early activity patterns can flag dropout, but provider-specific churn causes need analytics and interviews. [Frontline Learning Research](https://doi.org/10.14786/flr.v13i4.1351)
67. **Question:** Which providers have trust?  
    **Answer:** Trust must be established by target-segment interviews. Existing large providers have broad reach, but reach does not prove trust for this job. [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/skills-reports/global)
68. **Question:** Can a new product complement instead of compete?  
    **Answer:** Yes, a credible opportunity is the transfer layer—role-relevant practice, feedback and workplace application—alongside existing content/path providers; validate integration appetite. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
69. **Question:** What partnership incentives exist?  
    **Answer:** Providers and vendors benefit from activation, credentials, engagement and successful adoption; partnerships could exchange pathways, practice contexts or distribution. This is a business-development hypothesis. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)

## 6. Tools and technical ecosystem

70. **Question:** Which tools matter?  
    **Answer:** Map tools from actual recurring tasks: GenAI assistants, no-code/automation, data, APIs and collaboration products are categories, not a universal curriculum. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
71. **Question:** Which tools are available or blocked?  
    **Answer:** Availability is organization-specific and may be limited by privacy/security governance; screen approved tools, licenses, data rules and procurement. [NIST GenAI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
72. **Question:** What foundations are needed?  
    **Answer:** General digital literacy, data use/interpretation, prompt/task framing, output evaluation and responsible-use basics are more broadly relevant than advanced AI engineering. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
73. **Question:** What is durable versus tool-specific?  
    **Answer:** Durable capabilities include problem framing, workflow design, data judgment, verification, privacy and human oversight; tool menus and UI steps change quickly. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/ai-risk-management-framework-faqs)
74. **Question:** What can beginners do without code?  
    **Answer:** Current learning offerings include no-code, hands-on AI build courses; scope tasks to the learner’s role and governance constraints. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/customer-success-center/product-updates/all-updates/q1-26)
75. **Question:** Where do tools create friction?  
    **Answer:** Friction may arise from setup, cost, terminology, integrations and policies; no secondary source here ranks them. Use task observation and support-log review. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
76. **Question:** What concepts build confidence without engineering?  
    **Answer:** Focus on input/output, context, data, workflow logic, limitations, verification and risks; OECD finds most workers do not need advanced AI skills. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
77. **Question:** What does “build with AI” mean by segment?  
    **Answer:** It can range from a repeatable prompt and template to a no-code workflow or lightweight agent; define the minimum useful artifact per role. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
78. **Question:** What safeguards are needed?  
    **Answer:** Use data minimization, approved tools, human review, documented permissions, monitoring and response processes; NIST details GenAI privacy, IP and security actions. [NIST GenAI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
79. **Question:** How verify a real task?  
    **Answer:** Capture the artifact, task steps, rationale and quality review in a safe sandbox or simulated task; validate independent performance, not watch time. [Ford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/piq.20108)
80. **Question:** Which vendors already educate users?  
    **Answer:** Tool and learning vendors already provide pathways, hands-on practice and credentials; build a competitor inventory by tool category and target role. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)

## 7. Journeys and communication flows

81. **Question:** How does curiosity become resource selection?  
    **Answer:** Existing platforms offer goal-setting, role guides and learning paths, but the user’s off-platform journey must be reconstructed through interviews. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a1429531)
82. **Question:** Where do learners discover resources?  
    **Answer:** Market evidence shows large platforms, employer recommendations and tool vendors are channels; primary research should quantify social, peer and search discovery. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a700799)
83. **Question:** Who influences resource choice?  
    **Answer:** Employers/admins can assign or recommend content, but managers, peers and creators may be influential. Ask for the exact last choice and source of influence. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a700799)
84. **Question:** How do learners communicate needs?  
    **Answer:** No general evidence specifies the channel. Map the actual ask, channel, context shared, response time and result across managers, mentors and peers. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
85. **Question:** How do managers communicate expectations?  
    **Answer:** Enterprise platforms can distribute recommendations and due dates, but task-level safe-use expectations usually require local policy and manager communication. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a700799)
86. **Question:** How do providers detect confusion?  
    **Answer:** Behavioral traces can identify disengagement; meaningful confusion detection also needs task attempts, errors and direct feedback. [Frontline Learning Research](https://doi.org/10.14786/flr.v13i4.1351)
87. **Question:** Where is context lost between content and work?  
    **Answer:** Transfer literature identifies the work environment as a separate determinant, indicating a likely handoff risk between generic content and workplace use. [Blume meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880)
88. **Question:** How do learners share work and get feedback?  
    **Answer:** This is a primary-research gap; capture the artifact, channel, reviewer, turnaround and whether feedback changes the next attempt. [ACM Learning@Scale](https://doi.org/10.1145/3430895.3460991)
89. **Question:** How are templates and success stories circulated?  
    **Answer:** Existing platforms centralize content and organizational recommendations, but internal circulation practices differ; interview teams and inspect their channels. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a700799)
90. **Question:** What handoffs exist?  
    **Answer:** At minimum: discovery → selection → instruction → practice → feedback → application → evidence → next learning. Transfer research says the work environment must be included, not treated as an afterthought. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
91. **Question:** Which channels matter?  
    **Answer:** No global answer is defensible; identify channels by cohort and employer, then assess frequency, trust and response speed. [Microsoft](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/final/en-us/microsoft-product-and-services/ai/pdf/executive-summary-work-trend-index-annual-report.pdf)
92. **Question:** Where do channels fail?  
    **Answer:** Diagnose failures using concrete cases: incomplete context, delayed help, irrelevant advice, policy uncertainty or no owner. Secondary evidence does not rank these. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)

## 8. Alternatives and unmet needs

93. **Question:** What alternatives are used today?  
    **Answer:** Content platforms, AI assistants, colleagues, mentors, communities, employer programs and direct experimentation are plausible alternatives; quantify substitution in interviews. [Microsoft/LinkedIn](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part/)
94. **Question:** What does each alternative do well?  
    **Answer:** Courses structure content; AI assistants offer immediate access; peers offer context; employers offer access and legitimacy; pathways offer sequencing. Evaluate each against a chosen task. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/learning)
95. **Question:** What does each alternative fail to solve?  
    **Answer:** No alternative automatically guarantees transfer; the learner, training design and workplace environment all influence application. [Blume meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880)
96. **Question:** Which products solve motivation, structure, practice, personalization, accountability or application?  
    **Answer:** LinkedIn documents role-based pathways, practice, coaching, role play and credentials; treat these as competitor features, not proof that the underlying job is fully solved. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/customer-success-center/product-updates/all-updates/q1-26)
97. **Question:** Which Duolingo-like mechanisms have been tried?  
    **Answer:** This source set does not establish a complete landscape of Duolingo-like professional-learning products. Conduct a dedicated competitor scan; do not assume gamification equals skill transfer. [ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1290236.pdf)
98. **Question:** Why can completion fail to create capability?  
    **Answer:** Completion measures participation; transfer requires use in work and is shaped by content validity, motivation, support and environment. [IEEE](https://doi.org/10.1109/PCI.2010.40)
99. **Question:** Why do AI assistants not solve learning-by-doing alone?  
    **Answer:** They can offer immediate help, but trustworthy learning still needs verification, structured progression, safe practice and independent assessment. [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research?hub=83250)
100. **Question:** What workarounds do users create?  
     **Answer:** This needs behavioral research. Ask for the actual sequence of tools, people and resources used in the last attempted task. [Microsoft/LinkedIn](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part/)
101. **Question:** What do users pay for?  
     **Answer:** Existing products sell subscriptions, team licenses and credentials, but price sensitivity and payer differ by segment. Run willingness-to-pay tests after confirming a painful job. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/learning)
102. **Question:** What causes switching?  
     **Answer:** Likely triggers are a more relevant task outcome, faster progress, trusted proof, employer fit or lower risk; validate with switch interviews, not hypothetical preference. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
103. **Question:** Is there an underserved intersection?  
     **Answer:** Secondary evidence cannot identify one reliably. Search for a role × recurring task × skill level × constrained environment where alternatives fail and pain is frequent. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
104. **Question:** Which pains are well solved?  
     **Answer:** Generic content, role pathways, basic credentials and some hands-on practice are already served by major platforms; avoid rebuilding them without a differentiated transfer problem. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)

## 9. Learning experience and behaviour

105. **Question:** What format fits users?  
     **Answer:** There is no universal winner. Use short, role-relevant practice loops where time is scarce, then test against a real task; training transfer depends on design and environment. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
106. **Question:** What is the smallest meaningful practice activity?  
     **Answer:** A bounded task that produces an inspectable useful artifact and requires the learner to make/justify a decision is a strong testable unit. [ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1290236.pdf)
107. **Question:** What progression is needed?  
     **Answer:** A sensible baseline is understand → apply → build → specialize, with role-specific exit criteria; validate whether learners need every stage. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
108. **Question:** When teach versus learn by doing?  
     **Answer:** Teach only the minimum concept needed to attempt a task; combine explanation, practice, feedback and reflection rather than choosing content or doing exclusively. [EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/evidence-reviews/metacognition-and-self-regulation)
109. **Question:** What feedback helps after mistakes?  
     **Answer:** Feedback should be timely, specific to the attempted task, and paired with another attempt; predictive feedback has improved completion in a MOOC experiment. [ACM Learning@Scale](https://doi.org/10.1145/3430895.3460991)
110. **Question:** What personalization is useful?  
     **Answer:** Existing platforms personalize by role, level, goals and company skill data; test whether task, tool access and policy produce materially better relevance. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a1429531)
111. **Question:** What makes it habit-forming without shallow engagement?  
     **Answer:** Tie a repeat loop to relevant progress and independent task success, not only points; measure retained application separately from return rate. [ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1290236.pdf)
112. **Question:** Which incentives work?  
     **Answer:** Credentials, career pathways, visible progress and work outcomes are current market mechanisms; compare their effect on completed applied tasks, not clicks. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
113. **Question:** How prevent gamification substituting for capability?  
     **Answer:** Make rewards contingent on validated task performance, explanation and later reuse—not on streaks, lesson time or passive completion. [Ford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/piq.20108)
114. **Question:** What support should be AI, peer, expert or manager-led?  
     **Answer:** Use AI for immediate low-risk guidance; peers/experts for context and judgment; managers for opportunity and standards. Test escalation patterns and quality. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/ai-risk-management-framework-faqs)
115. **Question:** What edge cases arise?  
     **Answer:** Account for unclear goals, no work access, rapid progress, overwhelm, restricted tools and unsafe data. These are design hypotheses requiring usability tests. [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research?hub=83250)
116. **Question:** What if the task is outside curriculum?  
     **Answer:** Provide a safe triage: clarify goal, state limits, route to a broader framework or expert, and avoid unsupported high-risk advice. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)
117. **Question:** What accessibility/language/cultural needs matter?  
     **Answer:** They vary across markets; Coursera’s report shows substantial country/region variation and multilingual delivery is a market response. Choose geography before making requirements. [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/skills-reports/global)

## 10. Outcomes and measurement

118. **Question:** What is a learner’s measurable outcome?  
     **Answer:** Define an observable task a learner can independently complete, verify and reuse in their role; do not use self-reported confidence alone. [Ford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/piq.20108)
119. **Question:** What is an employer’s measurable outcome?  
     **Answer:** Select a role-specific business metric—cycle time, quality, adoption, risk incidents or output—not generic training completion. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
120. **Question:** What leading behaviours indicate capability?  
     **Answer:** Candidate leading signals: starts relevant tasks, makes independent attempts, uses feedback, explains choices, and repeats application. Early platform actions can also predict persistence. [Frontline Learning Research](https://doi.org/10.14786/flr.v13i4.1351)
121. **Question:** What distinguishes confidence from competence?  
     **Answer:** Confidence is self-report; competence requires performance against a rubric on a representative task, ideally independently and with quality/risk checks. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/ai-risk-management-framework-faqs)
122. **Question:** What is first meaningful activation?  
     **Answer:** A strong candidate is completing the first role-relevant, verified task—not account creation or a first lesson. Test whether it predicts retention and application. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
123. **Question:** What repeat behavior indicates retained value?  
     **Answer:** Repeated independent use of the skill on new relevant tasks is stronger than repeated content consumption. [Blume meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880)
124. **Question:** What work outcome can be attributed?  
     **Answer:** Measure a before/after task metric with context documented; avoid claiming causality from engagement alone. Small randomized or matched tests are preferable. [EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/how-micro-randomised-control-trials-can-generate-rapid-evidence-on-classroom-practice)
125. **Question:** How measure application without sensitive data?  
     **Answer:** Use synthetic/sanitized cases, learner-submitted metadata, artifacts without sensitive content, manager rubrics and self-reported task logs. [NIST GenAI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
126. **Question:** What predicts stuckness/churn?  
     **Answer:** Falling activity, repeated failed attempts, no action plan, no return after feedback and unresolved help requests are candidate signals; validate them. [Frontline Learning Research](https://doi.org/10.14786/flr.v13i4.1351)
127. **Question:** Which metrics can be gamed?  
     **Answer:** Lessons completed, time spent, streaks, prompts sent and certificates can all be decoupled from transfer; pair them with task-performance measures. [Ford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/piq.20108)
128. **Question:** What drives recommendation?  
     **Answer:** Likely a reliable, shareable improvement on a relevant task; test referral only after users experience verified value. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
129. **Question:** What drives employer renewal/expansion?  
     **Answer:** Evidence of adoption and work impact, plus governance fit and administrator visibility, is more defensible than completion alone. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
130. **Question:** What is required before claiming improved capability?  
     **Answer:** Predefined capability rubric, baseline, representative task assessment, follow-up transfer measure, and a comparison where feasible. [Ford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/piq.20108)

## 11. Testable problem hypotheses

131. **Question:** Which segment has the highest-cost gap?  
     **Answer:** Secondary research cannot answer this. Rank segments with a primary scorecard: task frequency × consequence × unmet need × access × willingness to engage/pay. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
132. **Question:** Why does the gap persist despite resources?  
     **Answer:** The strongest evidence-backed explanation is a transfer-system gap: content alone does not control learner readiness, practice design, feedback, supervisor support or work opportunity. [Blume meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880)
133. **Question:** What would disprove a hypothesis?  
     **Answer:** Define a falsifier in advance—for example, users already apply an alternative independently and report no meaningful task cost. Use observable behavior, not agreement. [EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/how-micro-randomised-control-trials-can-generate-rapid-evidence-on-classroom-practice)
134. **Question:** Which pain is frequent, consequential and underserved?  
     **Answer:** No secondary source can establish all three for a precise segment. Seek convergent evidence: repeated recent incidents, measurable consequence, active workaround and inadequate alternative. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
135. **Question:** Which actor can change the outcome?  
     **Answer:** The learner controls practice; manager controls opportunity/support; employer controls tools and policy; provider controls design/feedback. Select the actor with incentive and authority in the chosen wedge. [Ghosh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1108/JWL-08-2016-0079)
136. **Question:** What minimal intervention tests it?  
     **Answer:** Run a small guided role-specific task with safe data, feedback and a later independent task; compare completion and quality with the current approach. [ACM Learning@Scale](https://doi.org/10.1145/3430895.3460991)
137. **Question:** What assumptions are being made?  
     **Answer:** At minimum: users have recurring tasks, want to learn, can access tools/data, receive permission/support, can practice safely, and will pay or secure budget. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
138. **Question:** Which assumption is riskiest?  
     **Answer:** Usually that a specific segment has a painful recurring task and will change behavior for a learning product; test this before solution preference or feature design. [Ford et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/piq.20108)
139. **Question:** What should be learned first?  
     **Answer:** Identify a segment’s last high-friction task, current workaround, consequence, access constraints and whether a small guided practice changes independent performance. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
140. **Question:** How might researcher familiarity bias the problem?  
     **Answer:** It can overestimate tool access, jargon comprehension, motivation and safety. Use observation, diverse sampling and task evidence to challenge assumptions. [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research?hub=83250)

## 12. Additional discovery angles

141. **Question:** Which gatekeepers approve tool/data use?  
     **Answer:** Typically IT/security, legal/privacy, compliance, procurement and data owners; NIST recommends integrating GenAI use with existing governance, legal, compliance and risk processes. [NIST GenAI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
142. **Question:** Whose budget pays, and what triggers a yes?  
     **Answer:** Possible payers are individuals, managers, L&D and employer transformation budgets. The evidence does not identify a universal buyer; test budget owner, approval path and required proof. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/ai-and-skills_f843b352-en/full-report.html)
143. **Question:** Which named products have tried Duolingo-style professional learning, and why did each succeed/fail?  
     **Answer:** This evidence set documents role-based pathways, practice, coaching and credentials at LinkedIn, not a complete causal comparison of Duolingo-style products. A dedicated competitor case-study review is still required. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/customer-success-center/product-updates/all-updates/q1-26)
144. **Question:** Why has a well-resourced player not solved it?  
     **Answer:** They have addressed major components—role paths, content, practice, coaching and credentials—so the opportunity may be a specific unsolved transfer or workflow handoff, not generic AI education. Validate this against target users. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)
145. **Question:** Has “why” been chained three levels deep?  
     **Answer:** Not yet; secondary research offers candidate mechanisms, but each observation needs a causal chain tested through interviews, observation and disconfirming evidence. [Shukla review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518)
146. **Question:** Is the sample diverse beyond the researcher’s network?  
     **Answer:** This cannot be answered before recruiting. Set quotas across industry, seniority, employer type, geography, language and AI experience; report sample bias explicitly. [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/skills-reports/global)
147. **Question:** Does the problem differ by geography, and what geography is targeted?  
     **Answer:** Yes, skills and AI-maturity patterns vary across 100+ countries; however, the case-study document does not specify a target geography. Choose one deliberately before generalizing findings. [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/skills-reports/global)
148. **Question:** Who validates or benefits from demonstrated capability, and is signaling in scope?  
     **Answer:** Employers, managers, recruiters and credential providers can value signals, while the learner values career mobility. Credentials exist, but whether signaling is the product’s job must be an explicit scope decision. [LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/upskilling-and-reskilling/ai-skill-pathways)

## Research conclusion

The strongest cross-source pattern is not “people need more AI content.” It is that broad workforce demand and abundant learning options coexist with a transfer challenge: relevant practice, feedback, safe workplace access, manager support, and credible evidence of application. This is a **secondary-research hypothesis**, not a validated insight. The next step is to test it with a narrowly defined segment and a recent real task.

## Expanded evidence ledger: amendments and triangulation

This section adds evidence from the extended web search to the numbered answers above. It is deliberately explicit about evidence limits: a global, country, vendor, or platform sample does not establish what is true for the eventual target segment.

1. **Questions 1, 7, 11 and 45 — What is the ecosystem and which work is affected?**  
   **Answer amendment:** GenAI exposure is broad but uneven: ILO estimates one in four workers globally are in occupations with some exposure, and frames the predominant outcome as job transformation requiring human input, not automatic redundancy. Treat task exposure as a segmentation input, not proof that any role has a learning pain. [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-2025-update)

2. **Questions 12, 26 and 70 — How should role needs be differentiated?**  
   **Answer amendment:** ILO identifies clerical work as highly exposed and notes growing exposure in specialized digital roles including financial analysts, web/multimedia developers, programmers and investment advisers. This supports task-level, rather than generic “non-technical,” segmentation. [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/generative-ai-work-what-it-means-jobs-europe-and-beyond)

3. **Questions 3, 22, 71 and 117 — What determines access and constraints?**  
   **Answer amendment:** The World Bank’s framework is useful for ecosystem mapping: connectivity, compute, context/data, and competency/skills are complementary foundations. A product that teaches skills but ignores device/connectivity, local context/data and access will misdiagnose some failures as learner failure. [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/dptr2025-ai-foundations/report)

4. **Questions 4, 8, 49, 72 and 76 — What counts as usable competence?**  
   **Answer amendment:** The EU AI-literacy guidance requires context-sensitive measures based on staff knowledge, experience, education/training and the AI system’s context; it does not prescribe a universal certificate or individual test score. This supports role- and use-case-specific capability definitions. [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers)

5. **Questions 8, 47, 51 and 57 — How should employers train?**  
   **Answer amendment:** The EU’s AI Act Service Desk explicitly states that there is no single approach to AI competency; appropriate training depends on the system used and staff knowledge. This is direct evidence against one generic curriculum for all roles. [AI Act Service Desk](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/faq/how-can-companies-ensure-ai-competency-eg-employee-training)

6. **Questions 3, 28, 31, 72 and 73 — Which foundational skills are durable?**  
   **Answer amendment:** DigComp defines digital competence as confident, critical and responsible engagement with technology; its areas include information/data literacy, evaluation, innovation and identifying one’s own competence gaps. These are stronger durable-learning candidates than tool-specific UI steps. [European Commission JRC](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/oldpage-digcomp/digcomp-framework_en)

7. **Questions 5, 13, 22, 41, 42 and 105 — Why do adults not persist?**  
   **Answer amendment:** OECD’s cross-country adult-learning evidence identifies time from work/family as the most common reported barrier among adults facing barriers, followed by suitability and cost; employer financial support strongly predicts participation. This strengthens the time-and-workflow hypotheses but does not identify the priority segment. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/trends-in-adult-learning_ec0624a6-en/full-report/why-is-participation-not-more-common_73893b53.html)

8. **Questions 51, 53, 58, 119 and 129 — Why are employers a critical node?**  
   **Answer amendment:** OECD finds much adult learning happens at work/during work hours and employer support is a strong participation predictor. Therefore employer support is not merely a sales channel; it can be a causal condition for learning access and transfer. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/trends-in-adult-learning_ec0624a6-en/full-report/executive-summary_6b403d28.html)

9. **Questions 5, 22, 105 and 142 — Which design levers reduce participation barriers?**  
   **Answer amendment:** OECD identifies a package of training leave, financial incentives, employer engagement, quality assurance and flexible/modular delivery as mechanisms used to address time, cost and support barriers. This is evidence for testing modular formats, not proof that microlearning alone solves the problem. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/increasing-adult-learning-participation_cf5d9c21-en/full-report/component-5.html)

10. **Questions 33, 34, 42, 98 and 130 — Is course completion evidence of capability or career impact?**  
    **Answer amendment:** A randomized Costa Rica evaluation of curated, free short Coursera pathways found only 10% completed at least one course and found no significant labour-market effect roughly two years later. This is one context, not a general verdict on online learning, but it is strong caution against equating access or completion with outcomes. [Journal of Development Economics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304387824000348)

11. **Questions 23, 41, 42, 66 and 126 — What does the wider MOOC literature add?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Post-pandemic systematic reviews continue to treat engagement, motivation, performance and dropout as intertwined rather than a single “content quality” problem. Product instrumentation must distinguish entry, activity, practice, feedback use and task transfer. [MOOC review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S154810932400072X) and [dropout review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844023024271)

12. **Questions 59, 67, 80, 93 and 104 — What alternatives are already abundant?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Class Central recorded 5,300 new courses across four major MOOC platforms in 2024, and Google AI Essentials was its most popular newly launched course. The ecosystem has an abundance/selection problem as well as a content problem. This is market-inventory evidence, not product-effectiveness evidence. [Class Central](https://www.classcentral.com/report/most-popular-courses-2025/)

13. **Questions 39, 40, 62, 79 and 121 — Do provider outcome claims settle the capability question?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Coursera reports positive work impact among completing GenAI learners and positive experience with its AI coach, but these are provider-reported outcomes. They are evidence that outcome measurement is commercially salient, not independent proof of causal capability improvement. [Coursera](https://blog.coursera.org/introducing-courseras-2025-learner-outcomes-report-global-findings-show-measurable-career-impact-for-online-learners/)

14. **Questions 14, 51, 58 and 142 — Are managers motivated to upskill?**  
    **Answer amendment:** edX’s 2025 manager survey reports strong stated AI-upskilling intent and planned time investment. Because this is a vendor survey, use it as a lead for interviews about urgency and budget—not as a market-size or willingness-to-pay conclusion. [edX](https://www.edx.org/resources/leaders-embrace-upskilling-in-ai)

15. **Questions 11, 12, 17, 18 and 74 — What does observed real-world AI use show?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Anthropic’s platform data show usage concentrated in some occupations/tasks and more augmentation than automation in its 2025 sample. This supports identifying specific tasks to augment; it cannot represent all workers or tools. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-anthropic-economic-index)

16. **Questions 20, 47, 71, 117 and 147 — Why must geography and employer context be explicit?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Anthropic finds AI use varies across countries, states and enterprise/API versus consumer use; higher-adoption countries and business contexts do not necessarily use AI in the same way. Select a geography and employer type before making product claims. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-geography?type=research)

17. **Questions 37, 43, 73, 79 and 120 — How should task success and autonomy be measured?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Anthropic’s later Economic Index proposes tracking task complexity, required skill, purpose, AI autonomy and success. These are useful dimensions for event design, but the measurement approach is platform-specific and should be adapted carefully. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-primitives)

18. **Questions 16, 25, 30 and 43 — What does job anxiety evidence show?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Anthropic’s 81,000-user survey associates greater AI exposure and early-career status with greater displacement concern, while reporting productivity gains. The sample is explicitly not representative, so treat anxiety as a segment hypothesis to test rather than a universal acquisition message. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/81k-economics)

19. **Questions 7, 22, 117 and 147 — What broad access/readiness pattern exists?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Stanford’s AI Index reports growing organizational AI use alongside persistent education and access gaps. It supports not assuming that general AI adoption implies individual readiness. [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report)

20. **Questions 15, 23, 117 and 147 — Does AI education vary internationally?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Stanford’s 2026 education evidence shows wide variation in formal AI education and ongoing growth in AI literacy outside formal education. It reinforces the need to choose and study a local market. [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/education?facet=app&mode=light)

21. **Questions 20, 43, 50, 54 and 78 — What is distinctive about finance?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Financial-services literature highlights sector-specific content safety, legal/regulatory scrutiny, risk taxonomy and the need to scope acceptable inputs/outputs. Finance should not be treated as a generic knowledge-work learning segment. [Financial-services risk paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20086)

22. **Questions 20, 50, 52, 56 and 78 — What does finance require beyond generic AI training?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Finance research emphasizes explainability, adversarial testing, auditability and human oversight. These requirements alter what “safe practice,” proof of capability and employer value mean for the segment. [Financial-institutions survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21574) and [model-risk paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15668)

23. **Questions 18, 32, 43, 50 and 56 — What is distinctive about HR and recruiting?**  
    **Answer amendment:** A recruitment audit found gender-bias risks in model recommendations; whether this generalizes depends on model and deployment. It supports treating fairness, transparency, privacy and human review as core HR-learning constraints. [Recruitment-bias audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21400)

24. **Questions 57, 78, 141 and 148 — How does education/training regulation affect the system?**  
    **Answer amendment:** EU guidance classifies certain AI systems used to determine access/admission/assignment in education or vocational training as high risk. If the product ever recommends or allocates learners in consequential ways, this becomes a design and governance consideration. [AI Act Service Desk](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/education-and-vocational-training)

25. **Questions 12, 20, 117 and 147 — What does a regional labor-market study add?**  
    **Answer amendment:** An IMF Philippines study finds exposure and complementarity vary by education, urbanicity, sector and occupation. This is evidence that segment definitions must account for local labor-market structure rather than importing US/global patterns. [IMF](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2025/043/article-A000-en.xml)

26. **Questions 14, 22, 23, 101 and 142 — What is the clearest evidence on adult-learning incentives and barriers?**  
    **Answer amendment:** Improving job performance and employability are prominent motivations, while time, cost, relevance and employer support are documented barriers. Research should ask about these as separate variables; “lack of motivation” is too blunt an explanation. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/trends-in-adult-learning_ec0624a6-en/full-report/why-is-participation-not-more-common_73893b53.html)

27. **Questions 29, 35, 65, 87, 90 and 132 — What does the total evidence imply about the system?**  
    **Answer amendment:** The best-supported system explanation is conditional transfer: practice, feedback and content matter, but transfer also depends on work environment, access, supervisor support, policies and task opportunity. This remains a research proposition for the selected segment—not a finished problem statement. [Training-transfer review](https://doi.org/10.1002/hrdq.21518), [transfer meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309352880), and [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/trends-in-adult-learning_ec0624a6-en/full-report/executive-summary_6b403d28.html)

28. **Questions 131–140 — What can secondary research legitimately select?**  
    **Answer amendment:** It can identify plausible segments, constraints, existing alternatives and testable causal mechanisms.
