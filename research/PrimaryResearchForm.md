Revised per the grilling session — changes from your original 12-question draft: added screening (geography/language), added frequency, added an explicit shadow-AI/workaround question with permission-giving framing, split confidence vs. external proof, baked in a second-level "why" under the friction question (no live interviewer to ask it for you), and added scaffolded multiple-choice/checkbox options alongside the open-text questions so responses stay fast to give and easy to compare across ~20+ people. ## Form title
How non-technical professionals learn and use Tech & AI at work

## Intro / consent note
We're researching how working professionals learn, understand, and use technology and AI in their work. There are no right answers — we're interested in what actually happened, including what didn't work or what you'd rather not have to explain to your employer. Responses are used for research purposes only; if a question feels too specific to identify you or your company, feel free to describe it in general terms instead of naming names. Takes about 8–10 minutes.

## Notes for you (not part of the form)

- **Recruitment/sampling:** the grilling session flagged this as still open — before sending this out, set explicit quotas (minimum N per industry, per experience band, per company size) so you don't end up with 20 responses that are all from your own network's industry/seniority cluster.
- **Q16–17 placement:** kept mid-form (after the friction question, before help-seeking) rather than up top, so respondents are already in a candid, specific mindset before you ask about something they might otherwise sanitize.
- **Manager/employer-side research:** this form stays employee-only, per the scope-cut we discussed. If you want the employer/manager perspective, that's a separate companion form — worth deciding now whether it happens before or after you analyze this round's responses.
- **Willingness-to-pay:** intentionally not asked here — add it later, once you've validated the problem itself, so you're not collecting unreliable hypothetical pricing answers this early.
